## 1. Environment for SPH-factor generation (single assembly vs colorset vs full core)

### 1.1 Single fully-homogenized assembly with reflective boundaries gives trivial factors (mu = 1)

- DRAGON5 user guide, SPH: module, option `HOMO`: "keyword to specify that the SPH factors are uniform over the complete macro-geometry. This option is generally used with a complete homogenization of the reference geometry ... In this case the neutron flux (transport or diffusion) will be uniform, which allows the SPH factors to be obtained (one per macro-group) using a non-iterative strategy. For a given macro-group the SPH factor will be equal to the ratio between the average flux of the region and the surface flux if the SELE option is used otherwise the SPH factors are all set equal to 1.0 (no correction). The SELE option allows an SPH factor equal to the inverse of the discontinuity factor to be calculated." [S19, IGE-335 §3.11] — VERIFIED. This is the explicit statement that, for a single assembly fully homogenized into one region with reflective (fundamental-mode) boundaries and flux-volume normalization, SPH is the identity; only a Selengut (surface-flux) normalization produces non-trivial (ADF-equivalent) factors.
- Same guide, `STD` option: "flux-volume normalization for the SPH factors (default option) ... Using this definition, the averaged SPH factor is equal to one." [S19] — VERIFIED.
- Theoretical reason (Labouré 2019): with reflecting boundaries the SPH system is singular and "at least G additional constraints need be enforced. This result, first demonstrated in [2], is the basis for introducing normalization factors in the case of reflecting conditions on every boundary. ... if only one or no constraint is imposed ... convergence is never reached." [S10, §3] — VERIFIED. With one equivalence region per group, the G normalization constraints (phi_bar_g = phi_bar_ref_g) fix the flux and the balance equation is satisfied by mu_g = 1 (the homogenized cross sections already preserve the infinite-medium reaction rates). This last sentence is my derivation, not a quotation.
- Even with multiple equivalence regions (pin-homogenized Empire assembly, 2-D, reflective): "for completely reflected geometries (assembly model) the SPH method tends to compute an eigenvalue that is slightly different from the one reported by Serpent 2. However, this behavior does not indicate an issue in the SPH procedure as all the reaction rates are still preserved. Since no leakage is present with reflected boundaries, this simply indicates that the eigenvalue obtained directly from the reaction rates slightly differs from that reported by Serpent 2 ... originates from the necessary use of normalization factors with reflective boundary conditions in order to remove the singularity from the system [19]. When vacuum boundary conditions are used, normalization factors are no longer necessary and the eigenvalue from Serpent 2 can be exactly reproduced." [S1, §6.2] — VERIFIED (2-D assembly: SPH-diffusion 1.21360 vs Serpent 1.21378, −14.9 pcm; 3-D assembly with axial reflectors/vacuum: 1.14086 vs 1.14084, 0.0 pcm).

### 1.2 Factors generated in a reflective lattice/supercell and transported to the core: documented failure (INL, prismatic HTR)

- INL/EXT-17-41516 (Sen, Hummel, Hiruta 2017) generated SPH factors for a prismatic HTR with DRAGONv5 seven-block supercells with reflective boundary conditions and the INSTANT nodal solver, then applied them to a 2-D mini full core (SPHTR, vacuum boundaries, 8 groups). Results, unrodded: INSTANT without SPH 1.26405; with SPH (all nodes) 1.26152; with SPH (fuel+CR nodes) 1.26613; with SPH (fuel nodes only) 1.27066; Serpent 2-D reference 1.24569 ± 0.00002 — i.e. the supercell-generated SPH factors moved k_eff by only ~250 pcm out of a ~2000 pcm homogenization error, and restricting the regions made it worse. Rodded: INSTANT no SPH 0.731020; SPH all nodes 0.712640; SPH CR nodes only 0.723274; Serpent 0.75560 ± 0.00004. The authors' conclusion: "The impact of the SPH correction on the full-core eigenvalue results was very small in both rodded and unrodded cases ... further improvement can be expected if the method to preserve interface currents is implemented" and "A better agreement ... could also be obtained by generating transport corrected reflector cross sections". [S16, Tables 13–14, §4.2, §5] — VERIFIED.
- Same report, on the method: "Although the SPH method used here has a strong equivalence relation to the local average reaction rates, there is a weak relation preserving interface currents, unlike the Generalized Equivalence Theory [12], which imposes a discontinuity of interface fluxes ... Additional effort ... may be necessary [to] preserve interface currents in the SPH procedure described in this report." [S16, §4] — VERIFIED.
- The same report states the supercell rationale: "a seven-block supercell with reflective boundary conditions has proven to produce adequate results for the full-core calculations" (for cross sections, from earlier INL work [9,10]). [S16, abstract] — VERIFIED. The SPH exercise above shows this does not carry over to SPH factors.

### 1.3 INL's current practice: reference fluxes from the full 3-D core in the SAME geometry as the Griffin mesh ("non-fundamental mode" SPH)

- "The homogenized few group cross sections and kinetic parameters used in Griffin are generated from the full 3D geometry. The SPH equivalence technique relies on non-fundamental mode conditions [4] and is implemented as an intermediate step between the Monte Carlo calculation and low-order core calculations. A 3D macro-geometry is used to generate reference multigroup fluxes in Serpent, and SPH factors are computed via a Griffin SPH Preconditioned Jacobian-free Newton Krylov (PJFNK) procedure for each statepoint [5]. One key specificity of this approach is that the SPH factors can optionally be computed for only a portion of the macro-geometry (at minimum, the fueled region) and still result in a preservation of the k-eigenvalue and reaction rates [5]." [S9, §1] — VERIFIED. (Ref. [5] there is Labouré 2019 = S10.)
- Workflow step 2: "SPH factors are generated by the Griffin SPH Preconditioned Jacobian-free Newton Krylov (PJFNK) procedure for each statepoint, using the multiplication factor (k_eff) calculated by Serpent and reference fluxes stored in the equivalence library. Different options are possible in terms of where the SPH correction is applied, which can range from all regions in the core to individually selected regions, such as fuel assemblies or specific fuel-containing elements. As long as all fuel-containing regions are SPH corrected, the eigenvalue, power distribution, and total leakage out of SPH regions can be reproduced [5]." [S9, §2] — VERIFIED.
- Same-geometry requirement (INL drum study): "Two Serpent models are used in this analysis. The first is a conventionally built model. The second takes the first model and sub-divides it into separate universes that match the discretization of the deterministic Griffin model. The second model is used to generate multigroup cross sections and reference fluxes for the SPH factor calculation. ... The software stack being developed with Griffin includes scripts that generate the mesh for the deterministic calculation and the appropriate Serpent model modifications. ... Table 3 compares the core eigenvalue as a function of drum rotation for both Serpent models. The two models are equivalent within Monte Carlo uncertainty." [S8, §2.2] — VERIFIED.
- Griffin training (eVinci-like microreactors): "Meshing done along with detector definition to ensure the SPH regions are ordered in the same way MC tallies are"; "Coarse mesh contains XS (or material) IDs and flux (or equivalence) IDs"; "State points capture the XS and flux dependency on various parameters (Tfuel, Tmod, control drum angle, etc.)"; "Need to generate the SPH factors at each state point before using them"; "Current eVinci models use Two-Step Homogeneous SPH Diffusion approach". The `[Equivalence]` block shown: `type = SPH`, `transport_system = diff`, `equivalence_library = '<fluxes>.xml'`, `library_name = 'empire_core_modified_11G'`, `compute_factors = true`, `equivalence_grid_names = 'Tfuel Tmod Trefl'`, `updated_equivalence_library = '<SPH library>.xml'`, `sph_block = '<SPH blocks>'`, then a production run with `compute_factors = false`. [S7, slides 9–11, 14] — VERIFIED. (The slides do not state the size of the SPH regions for the eVinci-like models.)
- VTB VTR (assembly-level, SFR): "Cross section spatial homogenization and group condensation using the flux solution from a high-order transport calculation. Typically, for advanced reactors, a 3D continuous-energy Monte Carlo transport calculation is employed to generate the multigroup cross sections and reference fluxes for the second step. ... Low-order transport solution ... with an optional equivalence step that recaptures the homogenization error as well as the remaining spatial/angular/energy discretization errors. In this model, the Continuous Finite Element Method (CFEM) multigroup diffusion approximation with SPH equivalence is employed [Labouré 2019]." "These reference fluxes phi_ref_{m,g} and cross-section sets Sigma_ref_{m,g} are generated by Serpent for different combinations of perturbations ... statepoints." "As for the Serpent VTR model, each driver fuel assembly is split uniformly in five axial layers. Each axial layer has a separate set of cross sections coming from the Serpent calculation." 6-group structure (ECCO-33 collapsed). "The SPH factors are then computed by Griffin at the selected statepoints and saved in the cross-section library." [S6e, vtr_model.md] — VERIFIED. Equivalence regions = assembly x axial layer (one per homogenization region); the Griffin input uses `transport_system = CFEM-Diffusion`, `equivalence_library = 'sph/vtr_sph_verification.xml'`, `compute_factors = false`, grid `tfuel tcool cr` [S6e, griffin_only.i] — VERIFIED.

### 1.4 Empire microreactor: what INL/ANL actually did (pin-cell-level SPH, 2-D core, 15 groups)

- Reference: Serpent 2 (ENDF/B-VII); "INL generated cross sections directly with Serpent 2 and converted to XML format using ISOXML using a different group structure for the SPH-Diffusion calculations" — a 15-group structure (Table 4-3: upper bounds 2.0E7, 8.21E5, 1.83E5, 4.90E4, 4.54E2, 4.81E1, 9.88, 4.00, 3.30, 1.00, 0.91, 0.32, 0.22, 0.067, 0.030 eV) "determined to capture the key structural aspects of the neutron spectra, based on solutions for the infinite lattice 3-D assembly model". Cross sections by composition (fuel pin, moderator pin, heat pipe, air gap/hole, monolith, reflector, control absorber), fuel split by assembly location. [S1, §4.1] — VERIFIED.
- Homogenization regions: pin cells ("pin-homogenized models together with SPH factors"; INL meshing framework "updated to support pin-cell homogenization for hexagonal geometries"; 2-D assembly 1,560 elements, 2-D core 78,583 elements; in 3-D, 6 axial SPH zones per pin). [S1, §1, §4.2, §6.2] — VERIFIED. NOT assembly-level.
- Serpent model modified for the SPH runs: "slightly reduced flat-to-flat distance to ensure that the peripheral pin-cells were not cut ... The axial reflectors for the 3-D assembly used in the SPH calculations are 20 cm at the bottom and 10 cm at the top". [S1, §3] — VERIFIED.
- Tallies: reaction rates compared two ways — "(1) based on the reference cross sections and fluxes provided to Griffin, which is computed for each SPH region and (2) based on direct tallies from Serpent 2, computed for each pin". [S1, §6.2] — VERIFIED. Per S13 (Shift version of the same workflow): "Run Shift to calculate the MC reaction rates and pin-wise fluxes needed for Griffin ... write these and the pin-wise fluxes to an ISOXML XML file. Run Griffin ... to calculate SPH factors. Run Griffin diffusion with the updated ISOXML XML file that includes the generated SPH factors". [S13, §3.3] — VERIFIED.
- Results (SPH-diffusion, Griffin): 2-D core, control drums in: k = 1.14519 vs Serpent 1.14519 ± 5.4E-6 (0.0 pcm), pin power RMS 0.56 %, max 1.13 %, min −1.06 % (direct-tally comparison), 172 s on 40 procs; drums out: 1.17242 vs 1.17242 ± 5.1E-6 (0.0 pcm), RMS 0.61 %, max 1.39 %, min −1.26 %, 154 s. 3-D assembly: 1.14086 vs 1.14084 (0.0 pcm), RMS 0.06 %. 2-D assembly: −14.9 pcm, RMS 0.31 %. [S1, Tables 6-1 to 6-4] — VERIFIED. Drum worth is not tabulated as such; because both drum states are tabulation points whose k_eff is reproduced exactly, the integral worth between the two tabulated states (Serpent: 1/1.14519 − 1/1.17242 = −2028 pcm in reactivity) is reproduced by construction.
- Caveats stated by the authors: "The SPH correction was constrained to the fuel pins only, because it could not be converged with the moderator and heat pipe pin cells, probably for the same reason [non-optimal spatial zoning of cross sections]." "No 3-D core SPH-Diffusion calculations were performed due to a lack of time and funds". [S1, §6.3] — VERIFIED.
- Future-work remark: "The SPH approach has been often applied to homogenized assembly geometry problems, together with simple diffusion calculations. The generation of cross sections and SPH factors may be complicated, requiring significant efforts for non-standard geometry cores ... A hybrid method with transport calculation and SPH adjustment may be able to provide robust and accurate solutions within a practical computation time." [S1, §8] — VERIFIED.
- Pandya et al. 2022 repeated the Empire workflow with Shift and Serpent fluxes (15 groups, "pin-ring" vs "pin-wise" cross-section sets): 2-D assembly, Serpent/Griffin, no SPH 1.28442 (+7064 pcm), with SPH 1.21360 (−18 pcm, pin-power RMS 0.32 %); Shift/Griffin no SPH +6920 pcm (PP RMS 1.34 %), with SPH −17 pcm (0.09 %). 3-D assembly: no SPH +7479 pcm; with SPH 0 pcm, PP RMS 1.00 % (13 XS sets) / 0.00 % (1628 pin-wise sets). 2-D core drums in: no SPH +6235 pcm (PP RMS 1.57 %), with SPH 0 pcm (0.55 %); drums out: no SPH +7161 pcm, with SPH 0 pcm. SPH factor ranges: 2-D assembly 0.43–1.52; 3-D assembly 0.09–7.88 (Serpent) / 0.16–24.37 (Shift); 2-D core 0.53–2.74 (in), 0.03–3.36 (out). "Convergence issues prevented these Griffin calculations from SPH correcting the heat pipe pins throughout the core in any case". [S13, Tables 6–14] — VERIFIED.
- Pandya et al. on why the uncorrected bias is so large: "with this 15-group structure, without the SPH correction, there is a 7% reactivity bias ... even with pin-resolved cross section sets, the eigenvalue result with diffusion (without SPH correction) still yields the 7% reactivity bias"; solver/energy/mesh sensitivity Table 15 (15-g diffusion +6920; SAAF-CFEM-P5 +5525; 252-g diffusion +6416; mesh-refined diffusion +4925; 252-g P5 refined +2204 pcm); SCALE homogenization study: 601 pcm (fuel-only homogenized) and 1522 pcm (fuel+moderator homogenized) "implies that this system requires significant correction through SPH factors to preserve local neutron balance". Abstract: "larger eigenvalue differences between heterogeneous and pin-homogenized solutions compared to those of traditional light-water reactor (LWR) designs, thus requiring super homogenization factor corrections for accurate eigenvalue and power distribution predictions." [S13, §3.7, abstract] — VERIFIED.

### 1.5 Heat-pipe microreactor (ANL HP-MR / VTB "MRAD"): SPH used only on the 3-D unit cell; full-core production models use heterogeneous DFEM-SN, not SPH

- ANL/NEAMS-21/3: "to our knowledge, it is the first time the Griffin 'Diffusion + SPH' method was applied for a heterogeneous geometry model in multiphysics transient simulations using bidimensional cross-sections tables." Applied to the 3-D unit cell: "every material block in the finite element mesh was an independent equivalence zone. Particularly, in the axial direction, equivalence zones were assigned for every 5 cm axial layer. In total, 208 equivalence zones were setup". Reference: "the region-averaged multigroup neutron fluxes were an input to Griffin in each equivalence zone. They were tallied using the Serpent-2 model at each temperature point and were converted to the ISOXML format by a user utility code"; "The reference k-eff is an import input parameter"; nonlinear SPH-diffusion solved by PJFNK. ## 2. Reflector, control drum, shutdown rod and other non-fuel regions

- Theory (Labouré 2019 §3.3): "provided that all the fuel regions are contained in D_SPH, the reference eigenvalue is still reproduced. This is true whether reflecting boundary conditions with normalization factors or vacuum boundary conditions without normalization are considered." "Since the reference fluxes in the non-fissile regions are potentially small (e.g. in the reflector regions), restricting these regions from the SPH procedure can be a good idea for at least two reasons: (i) if these fluxes come from Monte-Carlo, the uncertainty associated with them can be significant and may cause some convergence problems, (ii) if the numerical discretization of the diffusion equation is fairly coarse, negative fluxes may appear which, from experience, drastically hurts the convergence of the SPH procedure. In particular, if the problem has strong reflector and/or shielding regions close to the vacuum boundaries, restricting the SPH regions is likely to be necessary." Energy-wise restriction is also possible "by setting the corresponding SPH factors to one". [S10] — VERIFIED.
- HTTR application (Labouré 2019 §5.3): 99 homogenization regions (fuel stacks, control-rod columns, replaceable reflector, permanent reflector, instrumentation column, 9 axial slices), 10 groups, all-vacuum boundaries, 18 axial equivalence regions per column; "the fast fluxes [were found] to be negative in the PR on the sides and in the RR on top and bottom ... To avoid convergence issues in the SPH procedure resulting from such negative fluxes, the equivalence regions are restricted to exclude the PR and the upper and lower three slices throughout the core (i.e. a total of 732 equivalence regions are SPH corrected)". Uncorrected error average 1865 pcm; SPH with normalization 1526 pcm; SPH without normalization 0 pcm; power RMS 3 % -> 0.08 %. [S10, Tables 4–5] — VERIFIED. (Note: control-rod columns were kept in the SPH set; the permanent reflector was excluded.)
- Fission-production normalization: Labouré 2019 normalization factors are "applied to every SPH factor except that of the fission term"; with vacuum boundaries and no normalization the fission terms must be scaled by the reference eigenvalue ("requires to scale the fission terms with the reference eigenvalue"); the SPH-DF variant "does not require to scale the fission terms with the reference eigenvalue". [S10, §3, §5.2] — VERIFIED. No source found that discusses a specific pathology of fission-production normalization in non-fuel regions beyond the generic "small/negative reference flux" problem above.
- Empire 2-D core: SPH applied to fuel pins only; moderator and heat-pipe pin cells could not be converged [S1 §6.3; S13 §3.6] — VERIFIED. Control drums and reflector therefore uncorrected in that work.
- INL 2-D drum-rotation microreactor (5 MW, 192 U-10Zr elements, Al2O3 reflector, B4C drums): "The deterministic model uses SPH correction in the fuel elements and the first ring of the reflector"; the study is "the case where the control drum itself is not included in the SPH correction". Drum rotation between tabulated angles handled by linear interpolation of cross sections and SPH factors plus Griffin's cusping treatment; "All cases reproduce the eigenvalue at their SPH grid points. Cases 1 and 2 [grid 0/180 and 0/90/180 deg] underestimate drum worth in the first half of the rotation and overestimate worth in the second half. This same trend is seen to a lesser extent in Case 3 [0/40/90/140/180]." Serpent k_eff vs angle (0–180 deg, 10-deg steps) is tabulated (Table 3: 1.07308 at 0 deg to 1.07970 at 180 deg, total single-drum worth ~ 570 pcm); Griffin errors are given only as figures. [S8, §2] — VERIFIED.
- VTR (SFR, assembly-level): control-rod assemblies ARE SPH-corrected at the tabulated insertion fractions: "For the cases where the control rods are inserted, the Griffin k_eff without SPH equivalence are lower compared to Serpent. This is a result of using a homogeneous model where all the B4C-bearing pins are homogenized into one cross-section material, which doesn't capture the neutron flux self-shielding effects. Thus, preserving the rod worth requires the application of an equivalence procedure"; "An SPH correction corresponding to the fully inserted and fully withdrawn conditions is required in order to preserve the integral rod worth." Numbers: without SPH +213 to +263 pcm (rods out), −538 to −584 pcm (rods in); with SPH 0 pcm at all 18 statepoints. [S6e, vtr_results.md] — VERIFIED. (Whether the shield assemblies are in the SPH set is not stated on the page.)
- HTR-10 (pebble bed, MAMMOTH/Griffin predecessor, 10 groups): "the peripheral boronated bricks and the bottom carbon bricks pose great challenges for the Serpent code to obtaining cross section and flux tallies with reasonable statistical fidelity ... The poor statistics lead to problems with the SPH procedure ... we remove the boronated brick and bottom carbon brick regions from the MAMMOTH model and apply vacuum boundary conditions on all resulting external surfaces ... The vacuum boundary conditions reasonably approximate the neutronics behavior of the boronated bricks so that convergence of the nonlinear problem for computing the SPH factors is achieved." Results: diffusion 2520.8 pcm error -> SPH-diffusion 67.3 pcm (critical core); full core ARO/ARI/1RI diffusion +2543/+1061/+2379 pcm, SPH-diffusion within ~120 pcm, absorption-rate max error 0.536 %; control-rod regions were SPH-corrected (rod worth reproduced). [S15, §3.4, §4.1–4.3] — VERIFIED.

## 3. Normalization sensitivity (flux-volume vs Selengut; vacuum boundaries)

- Labouré 2019, abstract: "The need for this new class of methods arises from the inability of the current state-of-the-art SPH technology to properly reproduce the reactor multiplication factor for problems with significant leakage. This work shows that this defect lies in the use of normalization factors in the SPH algorithm: while they are necessary with purely reflecting problems to ensure uniqueness of the solution, they introduce homogenization inconsistencies if at least one vacuum boundary condition is present. Two solutions to this problem are presented in this work: (i) to simply remove the normalization factor or (ii) to introduce additional degrees of freedom in the form of DFs in such a way that the normalization factors can still constrain the problem." [S10] — VERIFIED.
- Labouré 2019 §1: "there is no theoretical reason to keep them with vacuum boundaries. Worse, the discrepancies observed in [6] for problems with this kind of boundaries can be directly traced back to these unnecessary factors." §3.2: "when normalization factors were introduced in [2], it was only designed to address the singularity of the system with reflecting boundaries and in no way intended to be used in other cases." "In summary, in the presence of vacuum boundary conditions and without normalization factors, if the provided eigenvalue k'_eff is equal to the true eigenvalue k_eff, then the sum of the leakage rates integrated over the vacuum boundaries as well as the region averaged reaction rates are preserved. Thus, the eigenvalue is also reproduced. However, this method cannot be exceptionally robust as it converges to the reflecting case without normalization factors as the size of the vacuum boundaries tends to zero. Its usage should therefore be confined to cases with significant leakage through the vacuum boundaries ... or if the SPH regions are being spatially restricted". [S10] — VERIFIED.
- Labouré 2019 §3.1 (reflecting case): "There are a number of possible normalization choices [6]. For the rest of this paper, the flux-volume normalization is adopted without loss of generality"; "with reflecting boundary conditions and normalization factors applied to every SPH factor except that of the fission term, the converged SPH factors do not depend on the multiplication factor used. Therefore k_eff can be omitted." [S10] — VERIFIED.
- Numerical magnitude (Labouré 2019): 1-D test: SPH with normalization and a vacuum boundary gives up to 70 pcm error, "this error follows very closely the behavior of the normalization factor"; 3x3 supercell with 2 vacuum boundaries (4 groups, Serpent k = 0.19418): no equivalence +1312 pcm (power RMS 5.9 %); SPH with normalization +414 pcm (RMS 0.022 %); SPH without normalization 0.0 pcm (RMS 0.015 %); SPH-DF +0.2 pcm; DF (DFEM) 0.0 pcm. HTTR: average 1865 pcm uncorrected, 1526 pcm with normalization, 0 without. Conclusion: "The SPH method with normalization should be reserved to problems with only reflecting boundary conditions"; "the least expensive approach is to use SPH without normalization factors whenever convergence can be reached and to use SPH-DF with normalization factors otherwise." [S10, §5.1–5.3] — VERIFIED.
- Empire (ANL/NSE-20/23 §6.2 and Pandya 2022 Table 6): reflective 2-D assembly −14.9/−18 pcm attributed to normalization; vacuum cases 0 pcm. [S1, S13] — VERIFIED.
- Selengut normalization: Hébert & Mathonnière 1993 abstract: "The second improvement is accomplished by the introduction of the Selengut normalization in the SPH equivalence procedure replacing the flux-volume normalization currently used with second-generation methods." [S18] — PARTIAL (abstract). DRAGON5 `SELE` options normalize the macro-flux to the reference surface flux ("Using this definition, the averaged SPH factor is equal to mu_bar = phi_bar_ref / phi_gap_ref"), and for a single-region homogenization "The SELE option allows an SPH factor equal to the inverse of the discontinuity factor to be calculated". [S19 §3.11] — VERIFIED. Hébert 2015: "The SPH approach underwent many improvements over years, mainly related to the normalization of the correction factors in each macro-group. A noticeable contribution was made by Yamamoto who found that the Selengut normalization can be applied a posteriori, after convergence of the correction factors." [S20] — VERIFIED.
- DRAGON5 on leakage vs normalization: "A normalization condition must be set if the macro-geometry has no boundary leakage (fundamental mode condition). If boundary leakage is present, no normalization condition is used but the SPH iterations are difficult to converge in this case." [S19 §3.11, options PN/SN] — VERIFIED.
- Griffin: the normalization options actually exposed in Griffin's `[Equivalence]` block could not be checked (griffin-docs.hpc.inl.gov unreachable). The published INL applications with vacuum boundaries (Empire, HTTR, VTR, HTR-10) use the "no normalization, fission scaled by reference k_eff" variant per S10/S1. — PARTIAL (inferred from S1 §6.2 wording and S10).

## 4. SPH alone versus assembly discontinuity factors / GET

- Hébert & Benoist 1991 (abstract): proposes "a super-homogénéisation equivalence technique between the flux and cross-section edit calculations to yield homogeneous diffusion properties consistent with the control rod worth calculations" for assembly-homogenized PWR diffusion. [S17] — PARTIAL. The SPH-with-Selengut-normalization = inverse-ADF equivalence is stated in the DRAGON5 guide (quoted in §1.1/§3) [S19] — VERIFIED; the 1991 paper itself could not be opened to confirm its own statement.

[S16] — VERIFIED.
- Labouré 2019: "The GET relies on so-called Discontinuity Factors (DF) to enforce preservation of the leakage rates at non-reflecting boundaries, and interfaces between any two equivalence regions ... Interior partial currents: "the partial currents at interior boundaries will not generally be preserved" by SPH. [S10] — VERIFIED.
- PJFNK-SPH paper (abstract): "a known weakness: the method does not preserve neutron currents well" — this phrasing comes from a search snippet, NOT from the abstract I retrieved, which does not contain it. [S11] — NOT VERIFIED.
- Empire (pin-cell SPH, 2-D core, same geometry): SPH alone reproduces k_eff exactly and pin powers to 0.55–0.61 % RMS / <1.4 % max [S1, S13] — VERIFIED. ANL's own assessment: "The SPH-Diffusion results are encouraging ... These differences are mainly driven by the less than optimal cross sections and can be further reduced." [S1 §7] — VERIFIED. This is the strongest "SPH alone is adequate" evidence for a microreactor, but it is pin-cell-level, fuel-only, 2-D, and at tabulated drum states.
- Drum worth between tabulated states (SPH alone + interpolation): under/over-estimation documented in S8 (figures only) — VERIFIED qualitatively.

## 6. Convergence behaviour of the SPH iteration

- Picard vs Newton (PJFNK-SPH abstract): "The traditional solution strategy for this nonlinear problem uses a Picard, fixed-point iterative process whereas the new implementation relies on MOOSE's Preconditioned Jacobian-Free Newton Krylov method to allow for a direct solution. The PJFNK-SPH can converge problems that were either intractable or very difficult to converge with the traditional iterative approach, including geometries with reflectors and vacuum boundary conditions. This is partly due to the underlying Scalable Nonlinear Equations Solvers in PETSc, which are integral to MOOSE and offer Newton damping, line search and trust region methods. ... Speedups of five times for diffusion and ten to fifteen times for transport were obtained when compared to the traditional Picard approach." [S11, abstract] — VERIFIED (abstract). ANL/NSE-20/23 §6.1: "The problem was recast into a steady-state source-free Newton formulation which accepts non-trivial solutions due to the non-linearity stemming from the SPH factors. This approach allowed to update these at every linear iteration, thereby achieving much faster convergence than obtained with traditional SPH iterations." [S1] — VERIFIED.
- Singularity without normalization as leakage -> 0: "the SPH method without normalization factors becomes singular as the leakage decreases"; in the 1-D test the un-normalized SPH fails to converge for low-leakage cases, whereas SPH with normalization and SPH-DF "show good convergence for any value of D_ref". [S10 §5.1] — VERIFIED.
- Negative / noisy reference fluxes in reflectors (negative fast fluxes in the HTTR permanent reflector; Monte-Carlo noise in low-flux regions) break convergence -> restrict the SPH regions [S10 §3.3, §5.3] — VERIFIED; same diagnosis for the far reflector of IPEN/MB-01 [S9] — VERIFIED; and for the HTR-10 boronated bricks, cured by cutting them out and imposing vacuum boundaries [S15] — VERIFIED.
- Empire: SPH could not be converged when moderator and heat-pipe pin cells were included (attributed to cross-section zoning) [S1 §6.3]; "Convergence issues prevented these Griffin calculations from SPH correcting the heat pipe pins throughout the core in any case (using Serpent and Shift cross sections). These convergence issues require further investigation." Newton iteration counts 4–18 (rel. tol. 1e-8 to 1e-14) [S13 Tables 7, 10, 14] — VERIFIED.
