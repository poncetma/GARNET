## 1. Defining condition, SPH factor, corrected cross sections

### 1.1 Reaction-rate preservation and the definition of mu

* [H15] abstract: "The superhomogénéisation (SPH) equivalence technique is a correction procedure based on equivalence factors. These equivalence factors are computed in such a way that a macro calculation made over a macro region and a coarse energy group with a simplified transport operator leads to the same leakage and reaction rates as a reference calculation performed without homogenization and with a fine group discretization."
* [H15] §2 Theory: "The SPH equivalence technique is based on the calculation of a set of equivalence factors {µ_{m,k}, m ∈ C_m and k ∈ M_k}, where C_m and M_k is a macro region and a coarse energy group of a full-core or macro calculation (see Sect. 4.4 of Ref. [6])." Ref. [6] = A. Hébert, *Applied Reactor Physics*, Presses Internationales Polytechnique, ISBN 978-2-553-01436-9, 424 p., Montréal, 2009. (The same sentence, citing "Sect. 4.4 of Ref. 1" = the textbook, opens [IGE335] §3.11.)
* [H15] §1: "In the classical implementation of the SPH technique, all cross sections and diffusion coefficients are multiplied by the equivalence factors. In order to preserve the reference reaction rates, the corresponding fluxes are divided by the same factors. This approach is consistent with a macro-calculation based on the diffusion theory or, more generally, with the P1 form of the transport equation."
* [L19] Eq. (4)-(6) (verbatim): "The SPH factors µ are used to correct the (removal, fission and scattering) cross-sections in each equivalence region m ∈ {1,...,M} and energy group g [4, 5]: Σ_{m,g} = µ_{m,g} Σ^ref_{m,g}, (4) in such a way that, for all (m,g), the reaction rates from the reference calculation and from the diffusion solution – denoted respectively by τ^ref_{m,g} and τ_{m,g} – are equal: τ_{m,g} = Σ_{m,g} φ_{m,g} = Σ^ref_{m,g} φ^ref_{m,g} = τ^ref_{m,g}, (5) where φ_{m,g} and φ^ref_{m,g} respectively represent the integral of φ_g and φ^ref_g over the equivalence region of index m. Necessarily, the SPH factors need be defined as: µ_{m,g} ≡ φ^ref_{m,g} / φ_{m,g}. (6)" ([4] = [H93a], [5] = [H93b].)
* [O16] §3.2 (MAMMOTH/Rattlesnake, the Griffin ancestor): "In diffusion theory, the SPH corrected cross sections are defined for each macro region m and energy group g as: Σ̃_{m,g} = µ_{m,g} Σ^ref_{m,g} where the tilde (∼) superscript represents values from the macro calculation and 'ref' superscript represents reference values. The SPH factors are defined to preserve the reaction rate between the macro and reference calculations. They also need to be normalized for eigenvalue problems to obtain a unique set of solutions, usually volume weighed average fluxes for each energy group." Then "µ_{m,g} = φ^ref_{m,g}/φ̃_{m,g}" and the normalized factor "µ_{m,g} = (φ^ref_{m,g}/φ̃_{m,g}) (φ̄̃_g/φ̄^ref_g)".
* [T23] Eq. (3): "µ_{m,g} Σ^ref_{m,g} φ_{m,g} = Σ^ref_{m,g} φ^ref_{m,g}, where φ_{m,g} is the integral of the flux obtained from the Griffin diffusion calculation over the homogenized SPH region V_m."
* [IGE344] §7.3.2 Eq. (7.11)-(7.12): "Σ_{a,m,g} = µ_{m,g} Σ*_{a,m,g} = µ_{m,g} P*_{a,m,g}/F*_{m,g}" and "νΣ_{f,m,g} = µ_{m,g} νΣ*_{f,m,g} = µ_{m,g} P*_{f,m,g}/F*_{m,g}", "where the reference integrated fluxes F*_{m,g} are also obtained from the full-core reference transport calculation" (P* = reference reaction rates, F* = reference integrated fluxes).
* [R17] (DYN3D/HZDR) Eq. (1): "µ_{r,g} = φ^het_{r,g} / φ^{hom,norm}_{r,g}, where φ^het_{r,g} is the average heterogeneous flux in region r and energy group g obtained from the Serpent detailed calculations, and φ^{hom,norm}_{r,g} is the normalized average homogeneous flux in region r and energy group g"; Eq. (3): "Σ̃_{r,g} = µ_{r,g} Σ_{r,g}".
* [S17] (INL, HELIOS/INSTANT) Eq. (1)-(2): "µ^{g,(l+1)}_i = Φ̄^g_{Het,i} / Φ̄^{g,(l)}_{Hom,i}", "This calculated SPH factor is multiplied by the homogenized multigroup cross sections calculated by the lattice physics code: Σ̂^{g,(l)}_i = µ^{g,(l)}_i · Σ^g_i".
* [H93a] abstract (paraphrase from the ANS page): the second-generation SPH equivalence "is built to preserve pin-cell reaction rates and assembly integrated fluxes".
* Kavenoky 1978 is cited as the origin by every source ([H15] ref. [2] cites Hébert 1993; [L19] ref. [1] "A. Kavenoky, The SPH homogenization method, Lugano, 1978"; [O16] ref. [23]; [S17] ref. [13]; [R17] ref. (7)), but the paper itself could not be opened (see last section).

### 1.2 Which cross sections are scaled; scattering index; chi

* [H15] §2, case 1 (diffusion macro-calculation), equation written as
  `∇·J_g(r) + µ_g Σ_g(r) φ_g(r)/µ_g = χ_g/k_eff Σ_{h=1}^G µ_h νΣ_{f,h}(r) φ_h(r)/µ_h + Σ_{h=1}^G µ_h Σ_{s0,g←h}(r) φ_h(r)/µ_h`
  and `J_g(r) = −µ_g D_g(r) ∇(φ_g(r)/µ_g)`,
  followed by "In conclusion: • Diffusion coefficients and all P0 cross sections (including the total cross section must be multiplied by µ_g. • Scattering matrix terms Σ_{s0,g←h}(r) must be multiplied by µ_h. • Averaged and volume-integrated fluxes must be divided by µ_g."
* [IGE335] §3.11, Eqs. (3.42)-(3.43) are the same equations; conclusion: "Diffusion coefficients and all P0 cross sections (including the total cross section NTOT0) must be multiplied by µ_g. – Scattering matrix terms Σ_{s0,g←h}(r) must be multiplied by µ_h. – Fluxes (such as NWT0 and FLUX-INTG) must be divided by µ_g."
  => In Hébert's notation Σ_{s0,g←h} is the transfer FROM group h TO group g; the factor applied is µ_h, i.e. the factor of the **departure (source) group**, not the sink group. This is forced by the balance: the scattering source into g is Σ_h [µ_h Σ_{s0,g←h}] [φ_h/µ_h] = Σ_h Σ_{s0,g←h} φ_h, i.e. the reference in-scatter rate is reproduced when the macro flux is φ_h/µ_h. Equivalently, "all cross sections of region i, group h" (total, absorption, νΣ_f, κΣ_f, and the whole row of transfers out of h) are multiplied by µ_{i,h}.
* [O16] Eq. (5) (MAMMOTH source problem): `∇·µ_{m,g} D_{m,g} ∇φ_{m,g} + µ_{m,g} Σ_{m,g} φ_{m,g} = χ_g/k_eff Σ_{g'} µ_{m,g'} νΣ_{f,g'} φ_{m,g'} + Σ_{g'} µ_{m,g'} Σ_{s0}^{g←g'} φ_{m,g'}` — the same rule (source-group factor µ_{m,g'} on the scattering term, χ uncorrected, µ on D inside the divergence).
* [L19] Eq. (1)-(4): the SPH factors "correct the (removal, fission and scattering) cross-sections"; "Since the SPH correction will only apply to the cross-sections and diffusion coefficient, the superscript ref for χ_g will henceforth be omitted." => chi is NOT corrected. In [H15]/[IGE335] Eq. (3.42) χ_g appears without µ. In [L19] Eq. (15)-(16) and [O16] Eq. (5) the νΣ_f term carries µ (fission production rate preserved), while χ is untouched.
* [H15] §2 cases 2 and 3 (transport macro-calculations) show where the "multiply everything" rule breaks: for SPN macro-calculations "All P0 cross sections (including the total cross section in the even-parity equations) must be multiplied by µ_g. • The total cross section in the odd-parity equations must be divided by µ_g. • Scattering matrix terms Σ_{sℓ,g←h}(r) with ℓ even must be multiplied by µ_h. • Scattering matrix terms Σ_{sℓ,g←h}(r) with ℓ odd must be divided by µ_g. • Even parity fluxes (averaged and volume-integrated) must be divided by µ_g. • Odd parity fluxes (averaged and volume-integrated) are not modified." For non-PN transport (PIJ, SN, MOC) "the macroscopic total cross section is not modified" and within-group scattering is corrected as Σ̃_{s2ℓ,g←g} = µ_g Σ_{s2ℓ,g←g} + (1−µ_g) Σ_{0,g} (Eq. 3.47) and Σ̃_{s2ℓ+1,g←g} = Σ_{s2ℓ+1,g←g}/µ_g + (1 − 1/µ_g) Σ_{1,g} (Eq. 3.48). [H15] §3: "In the case of a transport-diffusion SPH correction, both classical and reformulated techniques are identical."

### 1.3 Macro flux vs reference flux

* [H15] and [IGE335]: fluxes "must be divided by µ_g"; [L19] Eq. (6) µ = φ^ref/φ; [O16] µ = φ^ref/φ̃; [R17] µ = φ^het/φ^hom; [D14] "µ = Φ^het/Φ^hom" with "Σ̃ = µ Σ, Σ̃ Φ^hom = Σ Φ^het"; [IGE344] Eq. (7.7) writes the normalization as Σ_m F*_{m,g}/µ_{m,g} = Σ_m F*_{m,g}, i.e. the macro integrated flux is F*/µ.
  => Consistently: **Phi_macro = phi_ref / mu, phi_ref = mu · Phi_macro**, and Σ̃ Φ = Σ φ_ref.

## 2. Diffusion coefficient in a diffusion macro-calculation

Every opened source that states a rule multiplies D by mu (same direction as the cross sections). No source applying D/mu or leaving D uncorrected in a diffusion macro-calculation was found.

* [H15] §2 case 1, exact wording: "J_g(r) = −µ_g D_g(r) ∇ φ_g(r)/µ_g" and "Diffusion coefficients and all P0 cross sections (including the total cross section must be multiplied by µ_g." [H15] §1: "all cross sections and diffusion coefficients are multiplied by the equivalence factors".
* [IGE335] §3.11 Eq. (3.43): "J_g(r) = −µ_g D_g(r) ∇ φ_g(r)/µ_g (3.43)"; "Diffusion coefficients and all P0 cross sections (including the total cross section NTOT0) must be multiplied by µ_g."
* [L19] Eq. (7), exact wording: "In practice, the diffusion coefficients are also corrected to better match the reference currents, as discussed in [6]: D_{m,g} = µ_{m,g} D^ref_{m,g}. (7)" ([6] = [O18]); Eq. (14): "Σ̃_{m,g} ≡ µ̃_{m,g} Σ^ref_{m,g}, D̃_{m,g} ≡ µ̃_{m,g} D^ref_{m,g}."
* [O16] Eq. (5): the SPH source problem is written with "∇·µ_{m,g} D_{m,g} ∇φ_{m,g}" (Griffin/MAMMOTH).
* [Z25] (KIT, PARCS): "D^Homo_L,new = D^Homo_L · µ0 – the updated homogenized diffusion coefficient, which has reciprocal relations with the macro transport XS" (listed with Σ_R,new = Σ_R·µ0 and Σ_F,new = Σ_F·µ0).
* [IGE300] / [IGE344] (DONJON NCR:/SCR: modules, keyword EQUI): "keyword used to select a SPH factor set in the Saphyb. By default, the cross sections and diffusion coefficients are not SPH-corrected." (i.e. when a set is selected both the cross sections and D are corrected). [IGE344] §7.1 DLEAK: "Derivatives can be taken with respect to a leakage parameter itself (D_{g,i} or Σ_{1,g,i}) or relative to factor µ in µD_{g,i} or µΣ_{1,g,i}. Note that factor µ is not a SPH factor because it multiplies only leakage-related parameters." (The leakage parameter is written as µD, again a multiplicative form.)
* Argument given by the sources: [H15]/[IGE335] simply state that "neutron balance is satisfied if the SPH correction is applied as follows", exhibiting Eq. (3.42)-(3.43). Reading Eq. (3.43) with the macro flux Φ_g = φ_g/µ_g gives J_g = −(µ_g D_g) ∇Φ_g: inside a region of constant µ, substituting φ = µΦ into Fick's law J = −D∇φ yields D̃ = µD, so the current of the macro-calculation equals the reference current for the same cross-section/flux scaling. [L19] gives the practical motivation "to better match the reference currents". Hébert's SPN rule (odd-parity total cross section Σ_1 ~ 1/(3D) "divided by µ_g") is the same statement seen from the transport side.
* Different treatments found: none for diffusion. The only "divide" rules are for odd-parity transport quantities in SPN/SN macro-calculations ([H15], [IGE335] Eqs. 3.44-3.48). Sources that are silent on D (they only write Σ̃ = µΣ): [R17], [D14], [S17], [M17], [C22]. Caution (my own remark, not from a source): if a code re-derives D from an SPH-scaled transport cross section, D = 1/(3 µΣ_tr) = D/µ, which contradicts the rule above; none of the opened sources does this.

## 3. Normalization

### 3.1 Why it is needed (non-uniqueness in a reflected problem)

* [L19] §3.1: "In this section, it is assumed that ∂D_v = ∅. In that case, there is potentially an infinite number of SPH factors that can satisfy Equation 11. Indeed, assuming that (Φ = (φ_g)^T, µ = (µ_{m,g})^T) is a solution thereof, then for any c = (c_g)^T ∈ R^G, ((c_g φ_g)^T, (µ_{m,g}/c_g)^T) also satisfies this equation. In other words, there are at least G independent solutions (Φ, µ) satisfying Equation 11, which means that at least G additional constraints need be enforced. This result, first demonstrated in [2], is the basis for introducing normalization factors in the case of reflecting conditions on every boundary. This is also verified numerically: if only one or no constraint is imposed (e.g. the normalization factor is a constant independent of the energy group), convergence is never reached." ([2] = Hébert, CEA-N-2209, 1981.)
* [IGE344] §7.3.2: "In fundamental mode conditions and in cases where Eq. (7.5) is used, an infinity of SPH factor sets can satisfy the reference reaction rates in each macro-group g. A unique set is selected with the application of an arbitrary normalization condition."
* [IGE335] §3.11.2, keyword PN (and SN): "A normalization condition must be set if the macro-geometry has no boundary leakage (fundamental mode condition). If boundary leakage is present, no normalization condition is used but the SPH iterations are difficult to converge in this case."

### 3.2 (a) Flux-volume ("average flux") normalization

* [IGE335] keyword STD: "keyword to specify the use of flux-volume normalization for the SPH factors (default option). In each macro-group, the macro-fluxes in macro regions i are normalized using φ̃_i = φ_i · φ̄^ref/φ̄^mc where φ̄^ref is the averaged volumic flux of the reference calculation and φ̄^mc is the averaged volumic flux of the macro-calculation. Using this definition, the averaged SPH factor is equal to one."
* [IGE344] Eq. (7.6)-(7.7): "The simplest option is to use the flux-volume normalization condition which consists to preserve the averaged flux in the lattice. This normalization condition, satisfied in each macro-group g, is written Σ_{m=1}^M ∫_{V_m} φ̃_g d^3r = Σ_m F*_{m,g}", rewritten as "Σ_m F*_{m,g}/µ_{m,g} = Σ_m F*_{m,g}" (Eq. 7.7, 7.13).
* [L19] Eq. (12)-(13): "the flux-volume normalization is adopted without loss of generality. In that case, the normalization factors are defined as: η_g ≡ φ̄_g/φ̄^ref_g = (1,φ_g)_D/(1,φ^ref_g)_D, (12) where φ̄ is the average of φ over the spatial domain D, and are applied to the SPH factors as follows: µ̃_{m,g} ≡ (φ^ref_{m,g}/φ_{m,g}) η_g. (13)"; "Clearly, if that normalization is applied to every single term in Equation 11, the solutions are just as many as before. Instead, it is applied to every term except for the fission term". Result (Eq. 22): "η_g = k_eff/k'_eff ... the converged normalization factors are group-independent and only depend on k'_eff"; "(i) with reflecting boundary conditions and (ii) normalization factors applied to every SPH factor except that of the fission term, the converged SPH factors do not depend on the multiplication factor used. Therefore k_eff can be omitted."
* [O16] §3.2: normalized factor µ_{m,g} = (φ^ref_{m,g}/φ̃_{m,g})(φ̄̃_g/φ̄^ref_g), "usually volume weighed average fluxes for each energy group".
* [R17] Eq. (2): "φ^{hom,norm}_{r,g} = φ^hom_{r,g} · Σ_r V_r φ^het_{r,g} / Σ_r V_r φ^hom_{r,g}, where V_r is the volume of region r."
* [H93a] abstract: the second-generation method preserves "pin-cell reaction rates and assembly integrated fluxes" (this is the flux-volume normalization at assembly level).

### 3.3 (b) Selengut normalization and relation to discontinuity factors

* [H93b] abstract (ANS page, paraphrased by the fetch tool): the third-generation method "replaces the flux-volume normalization with Selengut normalization in the SPH equivalence procedure".
* [H15] §1: "The SPH approach underwent many improvements over years, mainly related to the normalization of the correction factors in each macro-group.[3] A noticeable contribution was made by Yamamoto who found that the Selengut normalization can be applied a posteriori, after convergence of the correction factors.[4]" ([3] = [H93b]; [4] = A. Yamamoto, M. Tatsumi, Y. Kitamura, Y. Yamane, "Improvement of the SPH method for pin-by-pin core calculations", J. Nucl. Sci. Technol. 41 (2004) 1155.)
* [IGE335] keywords (verbatim):
  - "SELE ALB keyword to specify the use of Selengut normalization for the SPH factors. It is necessary to know the averaged surfacic flux of the reference calculation. Two possibilities exist: • We use collision probabilities. We define the reference geometry with VOID external boundary conditions ... and to close the region for the collision probability calculations using the ALBS option. • We perform a flux calculation with the current iteration method in Eurydice. This option is only available if the interface current method is used and if keyword ARM is set in module ASM:."
  - "SELE FD keyword to specify the use of Selengut normalization for the SPH factors. It is necessary to know the averaged surfacic flux of the reference calculation. This value can be obtained by defining a small region near boundary in the reference geometry and by using the ADF FD B data structure ... In each macro-group, the macro-fluxes in macro regions i are normalized using φ̃_i = φ_i · φ^gap_ref/φ̄^mc where φ^gap_ref is the averaged surfacic flux of the reference calculation. Using this definition, the averaged SPH factor is equal to µ̄ = φ̄^ref/φ^gap_ref."
  - "SELE MWG keyword to specify the use of Selengut macro calculation water gap normalization for the SPH factors. [81] It is necessary to know the averaged surfacic flux of the reference and that of the macro calculations ... φ̃_i = φ_i · φ^gap_ref/φ^surf_mc ... the averaged SPH factor is equal to µ̄ = (φ̄^ref φ^surf_mc)/(φ̄^mc φ^gap_ref)."
  - "SELE EDF keyword to specify the use of generalized Selengut normalization for the SPH factors. [82] It is necessary to know the averaged surfacic flux and the averaged volumic flux in a row of cells of the reference calculation ... φ̃_i = φ_i · (φ̄^ref φ^gap_ref)/(φ̄^mc φ^row_ref) ... µ̄ = φ^row_ref/φ^gap_ref."
  - "HOMO keyword to specify that the SPH factors are uniform over the complete macro-geometry. ... For a given macro-group the SPH factor will be equal to the ratio between the average flux of the region and the surface flux if the SELE option is used otherwise the SPH factor are all set equal to 1.0 (no correction).   ([81] = R. Chambon, IGE-345 (NAP: pin power reconstruction module); [82] = T. Courau et al., ICAPP 08.)

* [D14] slide 4: "in case of zero currents across cell boundaries, SPH factors are the inverse of an ADF/CDF".
* [Z25] (describing Yamamoto 2004, secondary): "SPH factors are not directly calculated using neutron fluxes but rather with normalized neutron fluxes. However, Yamamoto observed that this normalization does not perform well in multi-assembly calculations where different assembly types are adjacent. The conventional normalization method does not account for flux discontinuities between assemblies, which increases homogenization errors in the peripheral regions of assemblies. To address this issue, Yamamoto developed an improved SPH method, where SPH factors are divided by an averaged 'cell-level' discontinuity factor obtained from each fuel assembly (Yamamoto et al., 2004)."

### 3.5 (d) Vacuum boundary: Labouré's argument

* [L19] abstract: "This work shows that this defect lies in the use of normalization factors in the SPH algorithm: while they are necessary with purely reflecting problems to ensure uniqueness of the solution, they introduce homogenization inconsistencies if at least one vacuum boundary condition is present. Two solutions to this problem are presented in this work: (i) to simply remove the normalization factor or (ii) to introduce additional degrees of freedom in the form of DFs in such a way that the normalization factors can still constrain the problem."
* [L19] §3.2: "This time, because of the boundary term, given a solution (Φ, µ), (cΦ, µ/c) with c ≠ 1 is not generally a solution of Equation 23, suggesting that no additional constraint is needed, i.e. that no normalization factor should be used. As a matter of fact, when normalization factors were introduced in [2], it was only designed to address the singularity of the system with reflecting boundaries and in no way intended to be used in other cases. It will be shown in Section 5 that doing so blindly may actually introduce significant errors. If no normalization factor is used, not only the reaction rates but also the reference eigenvalue should be exactly reproduced." ... "In summary, in the presence of vacuum boundary conditions and without normalization factors, if the provided eigenvalue k'_eff is equal to the true eigenvalue k_eff, then the sum of the leakage rates integrated over the vacuum boundaries as well as the region averaged reaction rates are preserved. Thus, the eigenvalue is also reproduced. However, this method cannot be exceptionally robust as it converges to the reflecting case without normalization factors as the size of the vacuum boundaries tends to zero. Its usage should therefore be confined to cases with significant leakage through the vacuum boundaries (see Section 5.1) or if the SPH regions are being spatially restricted".
* [EMP] §6.1 (Griffin practice): "for completely reflected geometries (assembly model) the SPH method tends to compute an eigenvalue that is slightly different from the one reported by Serpent 2. However, this behavior does not indicate an issue in the SPH procedure as all the reaction rates are still preserved ... This behavior is well-understood and originates from the necessary use of normalization factors with reflective boundary conditions in order to remove the singularity from the system [19]. When vacuum boundary conditions are used, normalization factors are no longer necessary and the eigenvalue from Serpent 2 can be exactly reproduced."
* [IGE335] (quoted above): with boundary leakage "no normalization condition is used".

### 3.6 When each normalization is appropriate (as stated by the sources)

* Pin-by-pin inside one assembly with reflective boundaries (fundamental mode): a normalization is mandatory ([L19] §3.1, [IGE335] PN/SN, [IGE344] §7.3.2); the default is flux-volume (STD), which preserves the assembly-integrated flux ([H93a] abstract; [IGE335] STD "the averaged SPH factor is equal to one").
* Assembly-level factors for a core calculation that also uses discontinuity factors / needs the assembly surface flux: Selengut normalization (SELE variants), third-generation SPH ([H93b]); for a fully homogenized assembly it gives µ = 1/DF ([IGE335] HOMO; [D14]). Yamamoto's a-posteriori Selengut rescaling ([H15] §1; [Z25] on Yamamoto 2004) addresses multi-assembly flux discontinuities.
* Assemblies containing reflector zones: STD FISS (flux-volume over fissile zones only) or ASYM (pin a reference mixture to µ = 1) ([IGE335]).
* Macro-geometry with vacuum/albedo (non-fundamental mode): no normalization ([L19] §3.2, [IGE335], [EMP]); alternatively SPH-correct the albedo function with an extra factor µ_{M+1,g} ([IGE344] Eq. 7.5) or add DFs on the vacuum boundary and keep the normalization ([L19] §4.2).

## 4. Iteration and convergence

### 4.1 Fixed-point ("SPH iteration", Picard)

* [R17] steps (a)-(d): DYN3D run with homogenized XS -> "The SPH factor µ for each region r and energy group g is then calculated by" Eq. (1) with the flux-volume-normalized homogeneous flux -> "The obtained SPH factors are used to correct the few-group cross-sections of each region r and energy group g as follows: Σ̃_{r,g} = µ_{r,g} Σ_{r,g} (3)" -> "The 3D rod/assembly model is calculated again using the DYN3D code with the corrected few-group cross-sections. Then, the obtained average homogeneous fluxes are used to calculate a new set of SPH factors. The iterative process continues for n iterations, and finished if the convergence criterion max_{r,g} |µ^n_{r,g} − µ^{n−1}_{r,g}|/µ^n_{r,g} < ε (4) is satisfied with the recommended value of 10^−5."
* [S17] §4: "Since the calculation of the SPH factors uses homogeneous fluxes that are not guaranteed to reproduce the reaction rates of the detailed heterogeneous models, it is necessary to adjust the cross sections iteratively until the reaction rates produced by the homogeneous model converge ... Steps (b) and (c) are repeated using Equations (1) and (2) until all SPH factors are sufficiently converged." (Figures 18-20 show eigenvalue, reaction-rate and SPH-factor convergence histories for a 7-node HTR supercell.)
* [IGE335]: ITER maxout = 200, epsout = 1e-4 defaults; MAXNB = 10 iterations with increasing error before abort; BELL acceleration (PIJ); FPSPH: with Anderson acceleration; "If boundary leakage is present, no normalization condition is used but the SPH iterations are difficult to converge in this case."
* [O16] §3.2: "The resulting system of coupled equations is non-linear, since the SPH factors depend on the flux calculation and, in turn, the fluxes depend on the SPH factors through the cross sections. The initial guess is set by using the macro region fluxes and k_eff from the reference calculation. The traditional way of solving this problem [25-31] employs a Picard (fixed-point) iterative process, which will be referred to as 'SPH iteration.' This approach was implemented within the MAMMOTH application with a dedicated solver containing an outer SPH iteration that calls the linear system solver, normalizes the fluxes and computes the updated SPH factors until the SPH factor convergence criteria is met."
* [Z25] (PARCS): "During each iteration, after a step is completed, the SPH factors for the next step are calculated based on the current PARCS flux and the reference Serpent2 flux. The XS for each region and energy group in PARCS are updated by multiplying these factors. The iteration continues until the differences in neutron reaction rates between PARCS and Serpent2 fall within a specified criterion."
* [M17]: "The convergence criterion was set to 10^-4."

### 4.2 Known convergence problems

* [O16] §3.2: "it is well established that equivalence theory applied to homogenized reflectors is difficult and the convergence of the problem worsens with more than two groups [33, 24, 34]. In addition, the presence of void boundary conditions can exacerbate the already poor convergence." ([33] Ragusa, Sanchez, Santandrea, NSE 157 (2007) 299; [24] Hébert CEA-N-2209; [34] Pautz, PHYSOR 2006.)
* [L19] §3.1: with fewer than G constraints "convergence is never reached"; §3.3: "if the numerical discretization of the diffusion equation is fairly coarse, negative fluxes may appear which, from experience, drastically hurts the convergence of the SPH procedure. In particular, if the problem has strong reflector and/or shielding regions close to the vacuum boundaries, restricting the SPH regions is likely to be necessary." Also: "choosing a value of k'_eff that is drastically different from k_eff (e.g. several orders of magnitude) may degrade numerical convergence so it is advised to use either an estimate of the 'true' k_eff or to set it to one."
* [O18] abstract: "The PJFNK-SPH can converge problems that were either intractable or very difficult to converge with the traditional iterative approach, including geometries with reflectors and vacuum boundary conditions."
* [IGE335]: see "difficult to converge" with boundary leakage, above.
* [S17] §4 on specificity: "the value of the calculated SPH factor highly depends on the type of discretization methods used for the calculation of homogeneous fluxes."

### 4.3 PJFNK-SPH (Ortensi et al. 2018) — unknowns, residual, performance

* [O18] abstract (verbatim, Semantic Scholar record of doi:10.1016/j.anucene.2017.09.027): "This work presents two novel topics regarding the Superhomogenization method: 1) the formalism for the implementation of the method with the linear Boltzmann Transport Equation, and 2) a Newton algorithm for the solution of the nonlinear problem that arises from the method. These new ideas have been implemented in a continuous finite element discretization in the MAMMOTH reactor physics application. The traditional solution strategy for this nonlinear problem uses a Picard, fixed-point iterative process whereas the new implementation relies on MOOSE's Preconditioned Jacobian-Free Newton Krylov method to allow for a direct solution. The PJFNK-SPH can converge problems that were either intractable or very difficult to converge with the traditional iterative approach, including geometries with reflectors and vacuum boundary conditions. This is partly due to the underlying Scalable Nonlinear Equations Solvers in PETSc, which are integral to MOOSE and offer Newton damping, line search and trust region methods. ... Speedups of five times for diffusion and ten to fifteen times for transport were obtained when compared to the traditional Picard approach. ... The SPH-corrected cross sections yield eigenvalues that are near exact, relative to reference solutions, for reflected geometries, even with reflector regions. In geometries with vacuum boundary conditions the accuracy is problem dependent and solutions can be within a few to a few hundred pcm. The root-mean-square error in the power distribution is below 0.8% of the reference Monte Carlo."
* Unknowns / residual (the full paper could not be opened; the following is from the INL documents that describe the same implementation):
  - [O16] §3.2: "The novel approach, referred here as 'PJFNK SPH,' involves the use of MOOSE's non-linear solver with the Preconditioned Jacobian-free Newton-Krylov method (PJFNK) ... The normalization of fluxes and computation of the updated SPH factors occurs at every linear residual evaluation, thereby accelerating the convergence of the algorithm. With this approach, it is important that the initial guess is within the Newton convergence radius. In order to improve the initial guess, the MOOSE MultiApp system is used to spawn an initial 'free SPH iteration' calculation with a limited number of iterations. The solution from this initial guess calculation is then transferred ... to set the initial guess for the PJFNK method. The SPH factors obtained from both methods were verified to be identical, within the convergence criteria."
  - [L19] §1 (describing [6] = [O18]): "the problem was recast into a steady-state source-free Newton formulation which – unlike linear, homogeneous problems with full rank – accepts non-trivial solutions due to the non-linearity stemming from the SPH factors." [EMP] §6.1 adds: "This approach allowed to update these at every linear residual evaluation".
  - [L19] Eq. (15)-(16): the equations actually solved are the CFEM weak form of the multigroup diffusion equation with coefficients µ̃Σ^ref, µ̃D^ref (normalized factors on all terms except fission), with the eigenvalue fixed at the reference (or supplied) k'_eff, i.e. a fixed-k source-free problem in which the unknowns are the group-flux nodal values and µ is a nonlinear function of the current flux iterate (µ_{m,g} = η_g φ^ref_{m,g}/φ_{m,g}). (Exact residual wording of [O18] itself: not verified.)
* Reported performance in [O16] §5.1 (TREAT 3x3 control-rod supercell, rod inserted, diffusion, vacuum axial BC): "a minimum of 1 and 4 initial free SPH iterations are required to converge the PJFNK SPH solutions for the 4 and 11 group problems, respectively. ## 5. Convention pitfalls: which way round is mu defined in each code?

Every opened source defines mu = (reference/heterogeneous flux) / (macro/homogeneous flux) and MULTIPLIES the homogenized cross sections (and, where stated, D) by mu. No source was found that defines the inverse ratio with division of the cross sections. The "divide" operations that do appear are: (i) the macro flux is the reference flux divided by mu; (ii) odd-parity quantities in SPN/SN macro-calculations ([H15]); (iii) Yamamoto's a-posteriori rescaling by cell-level DFs ([Z25] on Yamamoto 2004, secondary).

Bottom line for the "multiply vs divide" confusion: the direction is fixed by the definition. With mu ≡ phi_ref/Phi_macro (universal in the sources above) the cross sections and D are multiplied by mu and the macro flux is phi_ref/mu, so that (mu Σ)(phi_ref/mu) = Σ phi_ref. A code that stored f ≡ Phi_macro/phi_ref = 1/mu would have to divide the cross sections by f; none of the opened codes does this.

## 6. Scope: what SPH does and does not preserve; specificity of the factors

* Preserved by construction: region-and-group reaction rates ([L19] Eq. 5; [T23] Eq. 3; [H15] "the same leakage and reaction rates"). With reflective boundaries and flux-volume normalization, the eigenvalue follows from the reaction-rate balance, not from the reference k ([EMP]: "the eigenvalue obtained directly from the reaction rates slightly differs from that reported by Serpent 2"; [L19] Eq. 22). With vacuum boundaries and no normalization: "the sum of the leakage rates integrated over the vacuum boundaries as well as the region averaged reaction rates are preserved. Thus, the eigenvalue is also reproduced." ([L19] §3.2). [T23]: "As long as all fuel-containing regions are SPH corrected, the eigenvalue, power distribution, and total leakage out of SPH regions can be reproduced [5]."
* Not preserved: interface currents / surface leakage. [S17] §4: "Although the SPH method used here has a strong equivalence relation to the local average reaction rates, there is a weak relation preserving interface currents, unlike the Generalized Equivalence Theory [12], which imposes a discontinuity of interface fluxes in order to provide a degree of freedom to the coarse-mesh nodal calculations for preserving interface currents." [L19] abstract: SPH reproduces "reference reaction rates" while DFs are needed "to preserve reference leakage rates at desired surfaces"; "the inability of the current state-of-the-art SPH technology to properly reproduce the reactor multiplication factor for problems with significant leakage." [O16] §5.1: "the eigenvalues are not strictly preserved because of the vacuum boundary condition and the use of the diffusion operator, which becomes apparent in the leakage rate error." [O18] abstract: "In geometries with vacuum boundary conditions the accuracy is problem dependent and solutions can be within a few to a few hundred pcm."
* Environment / boundary-condition dependence: [N16] (PHWR): "Preliminary results show that the use of SPH factors does not yield more accurate results than standard homogenization ... The explanation appears to lie in the fact that SPH factors are dependent on node-boundary leakage." [M17]: SPH factors "can preserve very well the multiplication factor and the pin power but since they are calculated always for single assembly, some errors on the assembly interfaces remain [6]." [Z25] (on Yamamoto): "this normalization does not perform well in multi-assembly calculations where different assembly types are adjacent". [C22]: SPH factors generated per assembly type in single-assembly calculations; reflector factors from fuel-reflector configurations.
* Discretization / solver dependence: [S17] §4: "the value of the calculated SPH factor highly depends on the type of discretization methods used for the calculation of homogeneous fluxes." [C22] §1: "various errors from different origins, including the spatial homogenization, group condensation, spatial discretization, and low order transport approximation are simultaneously corrected [7]" (hence the factors are tied to the macro solver, mesh and group structure used to generate them). [H15] §2: the correction rule itself differs between diffusion, SPN and SN/MOC macro-calculations.
